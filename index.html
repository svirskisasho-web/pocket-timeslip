How it times

The browser gives GPS about once per second — far too coarse on its own. So the clock runs on the phone’s accelerometer at 60–100 Hz, and every GPS fix corrects that estimate’s speed and drift. Launch is detected from the accelerometer, so the start line is sharp to roughly ±30 ms; the speed curve is anchored by GPS Doppler, which is the accurate part of a GPS fix.

Expect within ±0.1–0.3 s of a 10 Hz Dragy box on 0–100, and better than that run-to-run. Good for A/B testing a map, fuel or tyres; not a timing certificate.

If the phone gives no motion events — the IMU chip reads off — it falls back to GPS-only timing. Launch is then taken from the first moving fix, backtracked to standstill, and the speed curve is resampled at 50 Hz with a monotone cubic before the distances are integrated. Slightly softer on the start line, perfectly usable: figure on ±0.2–0.4 s. Runs recorded this way are labelled GPS only on the slip, so never compare one against a fused run.

Before the first run

  1. Mount the phone rigidly. Vent or suction mount, screen up. Loose on the seat and the accelerometer fusion is worthless.
  2. Clear sky. Park out of tunnels and away from tall buildings; let it sit 2–3 minutes until ±<5 m shows green.
  3. Disable battery saver and keep the screen on. The app holds a wake lock while armed.
  4. Flat, straight, closed road. Run both directions and average — a 1 % gradient is worth about 0.2 s.
  5. Keep conditions constant between runs: same fuel level, tyre pressures, A/C off, windows up.
